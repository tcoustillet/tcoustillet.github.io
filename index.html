Short bio

Hello, world! I'm Thibaut. After obtaining a Bachelor's degree in biology and mathematics in 2019 at Sorbonne University (France), I completed a Master's degree in systems biology (bioinformatics) in 2021 at Sorbonne University. I'm interested in mathematical modelling as well as data analysis and algorithm design.

After that, I spent two years working in computational toxicology as a research engineer within the Systox research group at Paris Cité University, where I took part in the Neurophyto and PARC projects. My work focused on the exposome: I developed new approach methodologies (NAMs) and computational tools to understand the link between pesticide exposure in children and the socio-economic burden following neurodevelopmental disorders, as well as the link between exposure to environmental stressors and both breast cancer and Alzheimer's disease.

I then joined the NeAR team at Sorbonne University, where I spent two years working as an engineer in computational neuroscience. Cooperating closely with experimental researchers, I spent much of my time building computational tools that anyone can use, with no programming knowledge required. These included interactive dashboards to analyse calcium signals from neurons and respiratory time-series following spinal cord injury. The respiratory work was registered with the European APP (Agence pour la Protection des Programmes), and I'm a co-inventor on a patent covering a combined hardware and software device.

Over 4+ years of experience in academic research, I take the time to understand both the scientists’ needs and the data in depth before choosing an approach, so that the solutions I deliver are well-suited to the question and user-friendly.

Science communication is a real passion, and I strongly believe that good science is science that can be understood. That is why I took on the challenge of demystifying, once and for all, one of the most widely used (and most misunderstood) concepts in biology: the famous p-value. Click here to start your journey!
